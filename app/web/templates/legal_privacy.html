This policy describes what the software actually does. It is written to be read, not to hide behind. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer yet, and that review is listed as a required action in the project's HUMAN_TODO before any real customer is onboarded.

Who is responsible

The business that sends you a payment reminder is the one that holds your data — they are the data fiduciary. {{ app_name }} is the software they use. If you are a customer of theirs and want something changed, you can use the grievance link at the bottom of the payment page they sent you, and you can also reach us at {{ support_email }}.

What we collect

  • About the business using the software: its name, address, GSTIN and PAN if provided, phone number, billing email, and its invoice numbering preferences.
  • About that business's customers: business name, contact person, phone number, email, GSTIN if provided, address, and whether they agreed to receive WhatsApp messages.
  • About invoices: amounts, dates, GST split, payments recorded, and written-off balances.
  • About messages: which reminder was sent, on which channel, when, and whether it was delivered.
  • Security records: sign-in events, changes to settings, and webhook receipts, kept for audit.

What we deliberately do not collect

  • Card numbers, CVVs, or bank credentials. Payments happen on the payment gateway's own page, and we only ever receive a confirmation. If the gateway gives us a tokenised reference, we store the reference, not the card.
  • Aadhaar numbers, or identity documents of any kind.
  • Your customers' PAN numbers.
  • Anything not needed to send a reminder, take a payment, or prove what happened.

Why we process it

To perform the service the business asked for: raising and tracking invoices, sending reminders, accepting payments, keeping accounts, and meeting tax and record-keeping duties. Where we rely on consent — specifically, sending automated WhatsApp or SMS messages to a customer — the consent is recorded with its date and source, and it can be withdrawn at any time. Withdrawal stops the automated messages immediately.

Who else sees it

  • The payment gateway the business connects, to process a payment you chose to make.
  • A messaging provider, to deliver a reminder the business scheduled.
  • Our hosting provider, which stores the data.

We do not sell personal data, and we do not use it to train models or to advertise to your customers. Service providers are chosen for being able to do the job, and they are bound to use the data only for it.

How long we keep it

Invoice and payment records are kept as long as tax and accounting rules require, because deleting them would put the business in breach. Security and audit logs are kept for {{ log_retention_days }} days, and the logs remain within India as the CERT-In directions require. Message history is kept while the account is active. If a business closes its account, records are deleted or anonymised once the statutory retention period ends, and you can ask for export or deletion sooner as described below.

Your rights over your own data

If you are a customer of a business that uses {{ app_name }}, you can ask to see what is held about you, have it corrected, or have it deleted. Export and erasure are built into the product: the business can produce a machine-readable file with everything we hold about you, and can remove you. Ask them through the payment page they sent you, or write to the grievance officer listed there, and you will get an acknowledgement with a reference number. We aim to respond within {{ complaint_window_hours }} hours and to complete requests within 30 days.

When you ask for erasure, records that the business must keep for tax or accounting purposes are retained, and we will tell you exactly which ones and why. Everything else goes.

Security

Passwords are stored with argon2id hashing. Payment gateway keys are encrypted at rest and are never displayed again after being saved. Every webhook from a payment gateway is verified against a signature over the raw request body. Sessions use signed cookies that are invalidated when a password changes. Access to a business's data is scoped to that business, and every administrative action is written to an audit log.

If something goes wrong

Internet-facing incidents are reported to CERT-In within {{ cert_in_hours }} hours of being noticed, as the directions require, and affected businesses are told what happened and what to do. Where the law requires it, affected individuals are informed too. The incident runbook is part of the product's operations documentation.

Children

This is business software for invoicing and payments. It is not intended for use by children, and we do not knowingly collect data about them. If you believe a child's data has reached us, write to us and it will be removed.

Changes

When this policy changes, the version date at the top changes with it, and businesses using the product are told in the app. Previously recorded consent keeps pointing at the version that was in force when it was given.
